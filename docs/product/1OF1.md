# 1OF1 — product & business design

> **Google Play app. Generated art, exclusive to each buyer.**
> An existing, paying market entered on the axis you chose: supply and variety.

---

## 00 — What it is

**A wallpaper nobody else on earth has, generated for you, one a day.**

Every piece is produced by an algorithm from a 64-bit seed. When you keep one,
it gets a serial number and it is **retired** — that seed is claimed, marked to
your account, and never issued to anyone else again. Your home screen is
literally an edition of one.

Free tier: one generation a day. Paid: unlimited, plus the right to *claim* —
which is what actually converts, because claiming is the only way a piece
becomes permanently yours.

The name is the mechanic and the marketing in one token: **1OF1**.

## 01 — Why this one

You set four constraints. Here's how this clears each of them, and where it
doesn't.

### It's a need already being filled

Phone customization is one of the oldest continuously-profitable categories on
Android. Wallpaper apps, icon packs, widget apps and theme engines have been
making money for fifteen years without interruption. Nobody has to be taught
that they want this. There's no market education, no new behaviour, no "why
would I need that" — the Play Store has thousands of these and people install
them by the million.

### People genuinely want more variety, and the incumbents can't give it

The complaint in this category is *always* the same and it's structural: every
wallpaper app is a **finite gallery**. You scroll it, you find two you like,
you're done, you uninstall. The ones that added AI generation mostly produce
the same recognisable slop, and — this is the part that matters — **two users
who type the same prompt get the same picture.** There is no such thing as an
exclusive wallpaper today.

An algorithm with a 64-bit seed space has 18 quintillion outputs. Not
"thousands more." Effectively infinite, and provably unique.

### It matches the wedge you picked

You chose *generated, and exclusive to each buyer*. This category is the single
best fit for that wedge of the four I offered, because a wallpaper is an
**identity object**. It's the thing people see 90 times a day and show other
people by accident. Exclusivity has real emotional value on a home screen in a
way it doesn't on, say, a spreadsheet template.

### It's genuinely passive, in the only sense that's real

Marginal cost per user per wallpaper is **zero**. No sample licensing, no
stock-image rights, no GPU inference bill, no model API, no content moderation
queue, and no ongoing content production. You are not making wallpapers. You
made a machine that makes them, once.

The storage story is the nice part: because rendering is deterministic, **you
never store an image.** A user's entire collection of 400 claimed pieces is 400
seeds — 3.2 kilobytes. Your whole backend is a table of claimed 64-bit integers.

### And it reuses the one thing you've already proven you're good at

You have **50 hand-built colour palettes, 32 tokens each, already in this repo**
(`skins/*.json`), plus a documented import schema and a contrast floor you
already implemented once. Colour is the hard part of generative art — the
geometry is maths, but the *taste* is the palette. You already did the taste
work. Those 50 skins become 1OF1's 50 collections on day one.

You also already ship Android: `android/` with Gradle, working APK builds in
GitHub Actions, a WebView shell, and a licensing backend in `backend/`. This is
not a from-scratch project for you.

### Where it's weak — stated up front

**Subscription conversion in customization is harder than in health or
finance.** A wallpaper reads as a one-time need, so "why am I paying monthly"
is the objection you will actually face. The whole product design in §03 and
§05 exists to answer it: the daily drop makes it a habit, the claim-and-retire
makes it a collection, and a collection is a thing people keep paying to grow.
If you strip those two mechanics out you have another gallery app and it will
earn nothing.

The category also has a **cheap reputation**, earned by fifteen years of
ad-stuffed junk. The art direction in §04 is not decoration; it's the thing
that separates you from the reputation.

### Honest ceiling

Median solo developer on Play earns **under $1,000/month**. A realistic
top-quartile solo outcome is **$3,000–$15,000/month after 12–18 months** of
sustained work with tight positioning and subscription monetization
([source](https://nicheshunter.app/blog/app-ideas-indie-hackers-solo-devs-studios)).
Subscriptions beat ads decisively at this scale — $10/mo from 1,000 subscribers
beats ad revenue from 100,000 monthly users in most categories.

Health, fitness and finance verticals have higher ceilings than customization.
If the ceiling matters more to you than the fit, say so and I'll redirect —
but those categories cost more to build, carry liability, and don't use
anything you've already made.

## 02 — The mechanic: one of one

This is the product. Everything else is delivery.

```
GENERATE     A 64-bit seed → a deterministic set of parameters → a rendered piece.
             Free users get 1/day. Paid users get unlimited.

CLAIM        Paid only. Claiming writes the seed to your account and to the
             global claimed-set. It now carries a serial: 1OF1 · #000418 · FIELD
             It is yours. It is gone from the pool. Permanently.

RETIRE       Nobody else can ever be issued that seed. Not by chance, not by
             search, not by paying more. This is enforced server-side and it is
             the single sentence the entire product is sold on.

COLLECT      Your claimed pieces live in a grid with their serials and dates.
             The collection is the retention loop. People do not cancel
             subscriptions that hold things they own.
```

**Why this converts where a gallery doesn't:** a gallery gives you access. This
gives you *ownership*. The free tier lets you generate and set a wallpaper —
genuinely useful, genuinely free — but the piece stays in the pool and could
theoretically go to someone else. Claiming is the only thing behind the
paywall, and it's the thing people actually want once they've found one they
love.

**Be precise in the copy, because the claim is a real promise.** You are not
selling a certificate, a token, a blockchain entry or an investment. You are
selling exclusivity of issuance, enforced by your server. Say exactly that:
*"We will never issue this one to anyone else."* Don't dress it up as anything
more, and don't let the marketing drift toward implying resale value. That
drift is how apps in this shape end up in a policy review.

**Anti-abuse:** rate-limit generation server-side, not client-side; a claim
requires an authenticated account with an active entitlement; seeds are issued
by the server, not chosen by the client, so nobody can farm or "mine" desirable
seeds.

## 03 — The generative engine

The actual craft. Eight **series**, each a genuinely different algorithm — not
eight parameter presets on one shader. Variety across families is what stops
the output looking samey after the tenth piece, which is the failure mode of
every generative app that ships a single algorithm.

| Series | Algorithm | Reads as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **FIELD** | Curl-noise flow field, 40–90k streamlines, additive accumulation | Smoke, ink in water, hair |
| **STRATA** | Layered fBm bands with vertical displacement + erosion pass | Geology, rock cross-section, sediment |
| **BLOOM** | Gray–Scott reaction-diffusion, 2000 steps, palette-mapped | Coral, lichen, cellular growth |
| **LATTICE** | Recursive quad subdivision with weighted split rules | Architecture, circuitry, Mondrian-adjacent |
| **HAZE** | Domain-warped fBm, 3 octaves of warp | Atmosphere, dye, colour field painting |
| **ORBIT** | Strange attractors (De Jong, Clifford, Peter de Jong variants), 2M point accumulation | Long-exposure light, orbital traces |
| **FRACTURE** | Voronoi shatter + per-cell refraction offset | Broken glass, ice, crystal |
| **DRIFT** | Particle sedimentation with ordered dithering | Gradient with grit, risograph, dusk |

### The seed pipeline

```
seed (u64, server-issued)
  ├─ bits 0–7    series          (8 families, weighted — see below)
  ├─ bits 8–15   palette         (which of the 50 collections)
  ├─ bits 16–23  palette subset  (which 3–5 of that skin's 32 tokens, harmonised)
  ├─ bits 24–47  series params   (algorithm-specific, 24 bits of expression)
  ├─ bits 48–55  finish          (grain, vignette, aberration, bloom amounts)
  └─ bits 56–63  composition     (focal position, rotation, crop, scale)
```

Deterministic all the way down: same seed, same device resolution, same image,
forever. That property is what lets you store 8 bytes instead of 8 megabytes.

### The three rules that decide whether this looks expensive or cheap

Generative art fails in predictable ways. These are the fixes:

1. **Weight the series distribution, and curate hard.** Not all eight are
   equally good at all palettes. Ship a scoring pass: render a candidate at
   256×256, score it on luminance variance, edge density, and whether the
   subject area behind the clock/icons is calm enough to read text over. **If
   it scores badly, reroll before the user ever sees it.** The user should
   never be shown a bad one. This single step is the difference between "wow"
   and "eh, another one."

2. **Every piece gets a finish pass.** Raw algorithmic output looks like a
   screensaver. The finish is what makes it look printed:
   - Film grain, 2–4%, at native resolution — not scaled up
   - A very slight chromatic aberration at the edges (0.5–1.5px)
   - Vignette, 6–12%, off-centre
   - A subtle paper/dust texture at 3% multiply
   - Dither the final gradient to 8-bit properly (ordered Bayer or blue-noise) —
     **banding on an OLED phone is the single most visible cheapness tell**

3. **Design for the phone, not for the canvas.** A wallpaper is not a picture;
   it's a background with a clock on it. Every piece is composed with a
   **quiet zone**: the top 22% (clock, status) and bottom 18% (dock, nav) must
   stay below a luminance-variance threshold. The scoring pass enforces it. Get
   this wrong and every wallpaper is beautiful and unusable.

### Rendering, and the live-wallpaper problem

Write the art **once, in GLSL**, and run it in two places:

- **Static pieces** — a WebGL canvas in the existing WebView shell, rendered at
  device resolution, read back to a bitmap, handed to `WallpaperManager`.
  Straightforward, and it reuses the Android shell you already ship.
- **Live wallpapers** — these cannot run in a WebView. You need a native
  `WallpaperService` with a `GLSurfaceView`-style GLES2 context. **The fragment
  shaders port between WebGL and GLES2 essentially unchanged**, so the art code
  stays single-source; only the host differs. Budget one focused week for the
  native wallpaper service, and treat it as a paid-tier feature, which it
  should be anyway.

Live wallpapers, widgets and always-on-display are **things iOS cannot do**.
That is your genuine reason to be Android-first, and it should be said out loud
in the store listing rather than treated as a limitation.

**Battery is a policy and review risk.** A live wallpaper that drains the
battery gets one-starred into oblivion. Cap it at 30fps, drop to 10fps when not
in foreground, pause entirely on `onVisibilityChanged(false)`, and offer a
"motion: off / subtle / full" setting that defaults to **subtle**.

## 04 — Creative direction

The category's reputation is cheap. The art direction's entire job is to
contradict that in the first two seconds — in the store listing, before anyone
installs.

### Identity

**Gallery, not app store.** The reference is a print studio or an edition
house: a place that issues numbered works. Everything is quiet, so the art is
loud. The app chrome should look like matte museum board and behave like it
knows the work is the point.

The wordmark is `1OF1` set tight, with the middle characters as a true fraction
mark where the typeface allows. The app icon is the fraction ¹⁄₁ knocked out of
a single solid field — at 48dp on a busy home screen, one glyph beats any
illustration.

### Colour

**The app has almost no colour of its own.** Near-monochrome chrome, and the
current piece supplies every accent — extracted live from the palette that
generated it. When you're looking at a FIELD piece in `vaporwave`, the buttons
and the progress ring are that piece's colours. The interface wears the art.

```
--ink        #0B0B0C     app ground (true near-black, OLED-friendly)
--board      #141416     cards, sheets
--board-2    #1D1D20     inputs, wells
--rule       #2A2A2E     hairlines
--chalk      #EDEBE6     primary text — warm, never #FFFFFF
--quiet      #8A8A90     secondary
--faint      #5A5A61     mono meta only, never body
--accent     ← extracted from the current piece, live
```

Light theme is a real design, not an inversion: `#F2F0EB` warm paper,
`#FFFFFF` cards, `#0D0D0F` text. Gallery walls are white; half this audience
will want that.

### Typography

| Role | Family | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UI | **Inter** (variable) | Everything readable |
| Serial / data | **Martian Mono** or **JetBrains Mono** | Serials, seeds, dates, dimensions, series names |

**The rule that carries the whole identity:** the serial line is always
monospaced, uppercase, tracked wide, and it appears under every single piece,
everywhere in the app, at all times.

```
1OF1 · #000418 · FIELD · CLAIMED 12 AUG 2026 · 1440×3120
```

That one line does more brand work than any logo. It's the museum placard. It's
also, not incidentally, the thing people screenshot and post — which is your
acquisition loop, so make it beautiful and make sure it's legible at
screenshot scale.

### Motion

Restrained. The art moves; the interface does not.

```
--d-tap     90ms    press feedback
--d-swap   220ms    sheet, tab, state change
--d-reveal 420ms    a new piece arriving
--d-claim  900ms    the claim ceremony — the one exception
--ease     cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)
--spring   stiffness 380, damping 30    (press states only)
```

**The claim is the one moment allowed to be cinematic**, because it's the
moment money changes hands and the moment people screenshot:

| t | |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Press CLAIM. Button collapses to a dot. |
| 120 | The piece scales up 4% and everything else in the UI fades to 20%. |
| 300 | A hairline frame draws itself around the piece, corner to corner. |
| 550 | The serial types itself on, character by character, in mono. *(The one place per-character animation is correct — it's a number being stamped.)* |
| 780 | A single soft flash across the frame, like a page under glass. |
| 900 | Settle. Haptic: one firm `CONFIRM` tick, not a buzz. |

Everything else: fade and 8px rise, 220ms, done. No parallax, no card tilt, no
shimmer skeletons, no bouncing. The gallery is quiet.

### Copy voice

Curatorial and flat. Short. Never excited.

| Say | Never say |
| --- | --- |
| "Yours. Nobody else gets this one." | "Unlock EXCLUSIVE wallpapers!!" |
| "Issued 12 August 2026." | "Fresh drops daily 🔥" |
| "Retired from the pool." | "Ultra HD 4K amazing backgrounds" |
| "Series: FRACTURE." | "AI-powered wallpaper magic" |

## 05 — The app, screen by screen

Five screens. Anything more is scope you don't need to ship.

### 1 · TODAY *(launch screen)*
The day's piece, full bleed, edge to edge. No chrome over it except the serial
line at the bottom. Three actions on a swipe-up sheet: **SET** · **CLAIM** ·
**NEXT**.

`NEXT` is where the free/paid line lives: free users see "1 of 1 today —
come back tomorrow" with a *watch-an-ad-for-one-more* option; paid users get
unlimited. **Do not watermark anything.** A watermarked wallpaper is unusable,
so a free user gets nothing, tells nobody, and uninstalls. A clean free
wallpaper on someone's phone is a billboard.

### 2 · STUDIO
Where paid users steer without losing the surprise. Series picker (8 chips),
collection/palette picker (your 50 skins), and a **motion** toggle. Then
GENERATE. Deliberately *not* a full parameter editor — an infinite slider panel
turns a gallery into homework, and the magic is that it decides and you react.

### 3 · COLLECTION
The retention engine. A grid of everything claimed, each with its serial. Sort
by date, series or palette. Tap to re-set, share, or export at full resolution.
Empty state is a single line: *"Nothing claimed yet. Claim one and it's gone
from the pool forever."*

### 4 · SET *(the bottom sheet)*
Home screen · Lock screen · Both · Live wallpaper (paid) · Widget (paid) ·
Always-on display (paid, where supported). A live device preview showing the
real clock and icon positions over the piece, so the quiet zones are visibly
doing their job.

### 5 · PAYWALL
Comes up on the second CLAIM attempt, never at first launch. By then they've
generated a few and found one they want — that's the moment.

```
        Claim it and it's yours alone.

  ✓  Unlimited pieces, all eight series
  ✓  Claim and retire — nobody else ever gets it
  ✓  Live wallpapers, widgets, always-on display
  ✓  Full resolution export, exact to your screen
  ✓  All 50 collections

     ○  $3.99 / month
     ●  $23.99 / year        ← default. "$2 a month"
     ○  $49.99 once          ← "yours forever"

           [ Claim this one ]
      Cancel anytime · Everything you claim stays yours
```

That last line matters and it must be true: **claimed pieces remain claimed and
exportable after a subscription lapses.** You keep what you own. It's the
honest position, it removes the biggest objection to subscribing, and it
sharply reduces refund requests and one-star reviews.

## 06 — The money

### Pricing

| | Free | Plus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generate | 1/day (+1 per rewarded ad, max 3) | Unlimited |
| Claim & retire | — | ✓ |
| Series | 3 of 8 | All 8 |
| Collections | 10 of 50 | All 50 |
| Resolution | 1080p | Device-exact, up to 4K |
| Live wallpaper / widget / AOD | — | ✓ |
| Ads | Rewarded only, never interstitial | None |

**$3.99/mo · $23.99/yr · $49.99 lifetime.** Annual is the default selection;
it's the one that makes the business work. The lifetime tier exists because a
meaningful slice of this audience will never subscribe to anything, and $49.99
from them beats $0.

**Never show an interstitial ad.** In a category judged on aesthetics, one
full-screen ad undoes the entire art direction. Rewarded-only, user-initiated,
always optional.

### What the numbers have to look like

Not a projection — the shape you're aiming at, so you can tell early whether
it's working:

```
10,000 installs
  → 3,500 activate (set a wallpaper)              35%
  →   140 subscribe                                4% of activated
  →   ~$2.60/mo net each after Play's cut         (annual, amortised)
  →   ~$364 MRR

Get to 100k installs at those rates and it's ~$3,600 MRR.
```

4% is an ambitious-but-real conversion rate for a well-executed customization
app with a genuine paywall moment. If you're under 1.5% after 5,000 installs,
the problem is the paywall placement or the free tier, not the traffic — fix
that before you spend a dollar on acquisition.

### The payment plumbing — and a correction to your plan

You mentioned Cash App and PayPal. Here's how that actually works, and it's
better news than it was two years ago but it needs care:

**Inside the app, digital purchases go through Google Play Billing.** That's
the default rule and the safe path. Play's cut is 15% on the first $1M of
annual revenue for developers in the standard programme, and payouts land in
the bank account attached to your Play merchant profile. That's the account
that needs to be set up — **not** Cash App, **not** PayPal.

**However** — since Google's compliance with the October 2025 US injunction,
Play does now allow apps selling digital goods to US users to take payment
through third-party providers, and PayPal and Cash App are explicitly among the
methods that can appear
([overview](https://solidgate.com/blog/external-billing-google-play/)).
Two cautions, both important:

1. **The fee position is genuinely unsettled.** Google's stated service fees
   for external billing (figures around 9%/20%, and a separate 25% +
   your own processor's ~5% structure) have been reported as not yet in
   effect pending the injunction. Depending on which regime is live when you
   launch, external billing can easily cost *more* than Play's 15%. Verify the
   current numbers in Play Console the week you wire it up — do not build on
   the figures in this document.
2. **This is US-only.** Outside the US, Play Billing for in-app digital goods
   remains the rule.

**My recommendation: launch on Play Billing only.** 15% is fine, it's one
integration, it can't get you removed, and it handles tax, refunds, currencies
and renewals for you — all of which are real work you don't want on day one.
Revisit external billing once you have revenue worth optimising. Getting a
first app pulled over a payments-policy misstep is a much bigger loss than 10
points of margin.

Where Cash App and PayPal **are** straightforwardly useful: a **web
storefront** at your own domain, outside the app, selling the same lifetime
unlock. You already have the shape of that backend in `backend/` — Stripe and
PayPal checkout, webhook-issued license keys, signed entitlements. Outside the
app you are free to tell your users it exists. Reuse that code.

## 07 — Building it

Your existing stack does most of this. You are not starting from zero.

**Reuse:** `android/` Gradle project and WebView shell · GitHub Actions APK
workflows · `skins/*.json` as the palette engine · `backend/` licensing server
(entitlements, signed keys) — repointed at claims.

**Add:** a GLSL art engine, a native `WallpaperService`, Play Billing, and a
claims table.

### Step 0 — Prove the art before anything else
Two weeks, one HTML file, no Android, no backend.

> Build a single self-contained HTML file with a WebGL canvas that implements
> the FIELD series from [paste §03]: curl-noise flow field, 40–90k streamlines,
> additive accumulation, palette read from `skins/skrimpad-skin-*.json`. Seed
> the RNG from a u64 so output is deterministic. Add the finish pass — grain,
> chromatic aberration, vignette, blue-noise dither. Render at 1440×3120. Add
> a button that rerolls the seed and one that downloads the PNG.

Then generate 200 and look at them on your actual phone as wallpapers. **If
40 of the 200 aren't good enough to keep, stop and fix the algorithm.** No
amount of product design rescues art that isn't good. This step is the whole
project's go/no-go and it costs you two weeks.

### Step 1 — The other seven series
> Implement STRATA / BLOOM / LATTICE / HAZE / ORBIT / FRACTURE / DRIFT per
> [paste the §03 table] as separate fragment shaders behind one interface:
> `render(seed: bigint, width: number, height: number) → ImageBitmap`. Each
> reads its parameters from the seed's bit layout in §03. Shared finish pass.

### Step 2 — The scoring pass
> Add `score(seed)`: render at 256×256, compute global luminance variance, edge
> density via Sobel, and mean luminance variance within the top 22% and bottom
> 18% quiet zones. Return a 0–1 quality score and reject below 0.55. Then
> `issue()` which rerolls until a seed passes, max 12 attempts. Log the pass
> rate per series so I can see which algorithms are producing junk.

### Step 3 — Android shell
> In the existing `android/` project, add a WebView-hosted screen loading the
> art engine, rendering at exact device resolution
> (`WindowMetrics.getBounds()`), reading the bitmap back and applying it via
> `WallpaperManager.setBitmap()` with `FLAG_SYSTEM` and `FLAG_LOCK` options.
> Build the five screens from §05 in Compose around it, with the WebView as
> the render surface only. Material 3, dynamic colour **off** — our accent
> comes from the piece.

### Step 4 — Live wallpaper
> Add a native `WallpaperService` with a GLES2 `Engine` running the same
> fragment shaders, ported from WebGL (they should need only precision
> qualifiers and the `texture2D` call changed). 30fps cap, 10fps when not
> foreground, full pause on `onVisibilityChanged(false)`. Expose motion
> off/subtle/full, defaulting to subtle. Measure battery draw over an hour and
> report mAh.

### Step 5 — Accounts, claims, entitlements
> Extend `backend/` with: `POST /issue` (server-issued seed, rate-limited per
> account/day by tier), `POST /claim` (requires active entitlement, writes seed
> to the claims table with a UNIQUE constraint, returns the serial),
> `GET /collection`. Reuse the existing signed-license approach for
> entitlements. Serial is a zero-padded monotonic counter, not the seed — it
> should read like an edition number.

### Step 6 — Play Billing
> Integrate Play Billing Library: one subscription product with monthly and
> annual base plans, plus one one-time lifetime product. Server-side
> verification via the Play Developer API against the claims service, real
> handling for grace period, on-hold, pause and restore. **Claimed pieces stay
> accessible when an entitlement lapses** — enforce that in the claims service,
> not the client.

### Step 7 — Store assets
> Generate the Play listing assets: feature graphic 1024×500, 8 phone
> screenshots at 1080×1920, and a 30-second preview video. Screenshots should
> be real device frames showing real generated pieces with the serial line
> visible, not marketing mockups with text slapped over them.

## 08 — Getting it onto Google Play

Two operational facts that will shape your timeline. Handle them now, not in
month three.

### The 12-tester gate

If your personal developer account was created after **13 November 2023**, you
must run a **closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for 14 continuous
days** before you can even apply for production access
([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)).
The requirement dropped from 20 testers to 12 in December 2024.

What trips people up:

- **"Opted in" means installed.** A tester must accept the invite *and* install
  the app under the exact Google account you invited. Invited-but-not-installed
  does not count.
- **The 14-day clock starts only once your release is approved and 12 testers
  are actually opted in** — not when you create the track. If someone
  uninstalls partway and you drop below 12, you have a problem.
- **Invite 18–20 to hold 12.** Attrition is guaranteed.
- Production access is then a **review**, not an automatic unlock, and it
  considers whether the test looked real — genuine engagement and feedback, not
  12 accounts that installed and never opened it.

**Do this in week one, in parallel with building.** Ship a rough but working
build to the closed track early and let the clock run while you finish the app.
Otherwise it's two-plus weeks of dead time bolted onto the end of your project.

If your account is an **organization** account, or a personal one created before
13 November 2023, none of this applies and you can go straight to production.
Check which you have before you plan around it.

### Store listing (this is your only marketing at launch)

- **Title:** `1OF1 — Wallpaper Only You Have` (30 char limit; count it)
- **Short description** is the highest-leverage 80 characters you will write:
  *"Generated wallpapers, claimed and retired. Nobody else ever gets yours."*
- **The first two screenshots decide everything.** Nobody scrolls. Lead with
  the two best pieces, full bleed, serial line visible. Not a feature list.
- Keywords that match real searches: `wallpaper`, `live wallpaper`, `4k
  wallpapers`, `generative art`, `abstract wallpaper`, `home screen`, `widget`.
  Put them in the long description naturally; Play indexes it.
- **Ship the data-safety form honestly.** If you collect an account identifier
  for claims, declare it. A mismatch between the form and the app's actual
  behaviour is one of the most common causes of suspension.
- Content rating, privacy policy URL, and a real support email — all required,
  all easy to fumble at the last minute.

### Policy landmines specific to this app

1. **Don't imply investment or resale value.** "Exclusive to you" is fine.
   Anything sounding like a tradeable asset invites a different and much
   stricter policy regime.
2. **Battery.** Live wallpapers get scrutinised and reviewed harshly. Ship the
   fps caps.
3. **Ads policy.** Rewarded ads must be user-initiated with a clear value
   exchange. Never on launch, never full-screen unprompted.
4. **Subscription disclosure.** Price, period and renewal terms must be visible
   on the paywall itself, not only in a linked page.

## 09 — How this fails

Worth writing down so you can watch for it.

1. **The art isn't good enough.** By far the most likely failure. Everything
   else here is solvable engineering; taste isn't. This is why Step 0 is two
   weeks with a hard go/no-go before you write a line of Android.
2. **Nobody finds it.** Customization is a high-volume search category, which
   cuts both ways. Your acquisition loop is the serial line — people posting
   their one-of-ones — so it has to be screenshot-beautiful, and there has to
   be a share button one tap from every piece.
3. **They install, set one wallpaper, and leave.** The daily drop and the
   collection exist to prevent exactly this. Watch day-7 retention above all
   other metrics; if it's under 15% the loop isn't working and no amount of
   traffic will fix it.
4. **You get stuck at the closed-testing gate.** Avoidable. Start it in week
   one.
5. **You build the parameter editor.** The temptation will be strong. A full
   editor turns a two-second delight into homework, and it destroys the
   scarcity of the claim. Resist it.

---

## 10 — Order of work

| | | Why here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wk 1–2** | FIELD series + finish pass. Generate 200. Judge them. | Go/no-go on the whole idea |
| **Wk 1** | *In parallel:* open the Play account, push a stub build to closed testing, recruit 18 testers | The 14-day clock is the long pole |
| **Wk 3–4** | The other 7 series + scoring pass | |
| **Wk 5–6** | Android shell, 5 screens, static wallpapers | Shippable app exists |
| **Wk 7** | Backend: issue / claim / collection | The mechanic goes live |
| **Wk 8** | Play Billing + paywall | It can take money |
| **Wk 9** | Live wallpaper, widget, AOD | The paid tier earns its price |
| **Wk 10** | Store assets, data safety, policy pass, production application | |

Ten weeks to a live, earning app, and week one is two weeks of art plus one
afternoon of Play Console admin that saves you a fortnight at the end.
